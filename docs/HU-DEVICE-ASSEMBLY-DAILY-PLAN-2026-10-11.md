## What the daily does today, measured

120 days of the current dealer, from wall #1 (2026-10-03), dealt through the game's test hook:

- Parts to place: 1 part on 12 walls, 2 on 25, 3 on 55, 4 on 26, 8 on 2. Difficulty is a coin toss, not a climb:
  Monday, October 5 was a one-part "Only suction"; that Thursday, October 8, an eight-part heated high flow.
- 42 of the 120 are a nasal cannula. The twists mostly change where the oxygen comes from, which is the problem the
  brainstorm started from.
- Every joke twist (no oxygen, only air, only suction, the pump) puts a bottle or a pump by the bed, and every bottle
  build is one or two parts. So today a joke day is an EASY day. A stacked Saturday joke dealt for this plan came out
  as a one-part build. Saturday cannot be the hard day until a bottle can feed a harder build.

## Two words this plan uses

- A TWIST changes the wall: a new room, a different cart, a barrier, the order written another way. You already know
  how to play it, and the one line on the wall says what changed. A month can bring in several.
- A MODE changes what you DO or how you win: find what is wrong, pick the device yourself, keep it working while it
  moves. A mode gets a one-wall teach the first time you meet it. One or two a month, as David ruled.

## What gets built, in order

1. THE CALENDAR AND THE CLIMB. The year turns on at wall #23 by itself, at midnight Mountain on October 25. Every day
   before it deals exactly what it dealt before (a test pins walls #1 to #22). Each day gets its kind from the
   weekday and its twist from the month's table; the dealer re-deals until the wall sits in the day's band. If a day
   ever cannot be dealt, today's dealer deals it, so there is never a missing wall.
2. THE MONTH SHEET, before anything else is added. Tests hold every day of the month to dealing, solving, sitting in
   its band, and being different from every other day; the hub's Puzzles screen pictures the month. Each later piece
   is held by it the moment it lands.
3. MONDAY ROOMS: the bed in the middle, the far bed, the split rail, dead outlets. Rooms are data already; the one
   engine change is that the dealer looks for the nearest oxygen outlet on these rooms (only these, so no dealt wall
   changes).
4. TUESDAY CARTS: just enough, supply dumped everything, the labels fell off, expired (the test gains one reason: an
   expired part fails, and says so).
5. WEDNESDAY ORDERS: shorthand, read the chart, written beats verbal. The order is written differently; the check is
   the same.
6. THURSDAY BARRIERS: the sink, the sterile field, the curtain. New pieces with squares nothing routes through, parked
   clear of the solution the way the crowded wall's IV pole is.
7. MODE 1, FIND THE FAULT, and its teach wall. The dealer solves the wall, then breaks it with the faults the shift
   already uses (unplugged, tubing off, the wrong flowmeter, the wrong percentage, the sampling line off). Also two
   faults, and the hand-off (half the solution set down and taped).
8. SATURDAYS: twists that stack; a bottle that takes a real run to reach, so a joke day can be a hard day; the Halloween
   dressing. (Changed in the build, 2026-10-11: a humidified bottle build needs a cylinder with a DISS outlet, a new
   part; it moves to month two, and Nov 14 puts the bottle across the room instead.)
9. SUNDAYS: three walls from the shift's stories, one daily, one result card with the three walls on it.
10. MODE 2, THE GOAL, NOT THE DEVICE, and its teach wall. An order that names a target and takes any setup that meets
    it; a setup that works but misses the target fails with the reason (a non-rebreather on a patient who retains).
11. THE DAILY CARD: "Monday · The bed in the middle", and a "New on the daily" row under Training that keeps every
    teach wall, so a player who missed a day can find it.

Pieces 1 and 2 are the backbone. Pieces 3 to 6 are small and many. 7, 8 and 10 are the real work.
