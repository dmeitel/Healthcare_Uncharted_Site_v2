Accessibility

The aim is WCAG 2.2 at level AA wherever a page can meet it. Here is what every page is checked for, where the site still falls short, and how to tell me.

What every page is checked for

Before a page ships it is opened at nine screen sizes, from a small phone held upright or on its side to a wide desktop, in both the light and the dark theme. The check fails a page for text below the type floor, text or lines without enough contrast, sideways scrolling, controls hidden behind other things, and touch targets smaller than 44 pixels on a phone.

Every control can be reached with a keyboard and shows where focus is. Motion follows your device's reduced-motion setting. The rule for every page is that a picture carrying meaning has a text alternative and a decorative one is hidden from screen readers.

Where it falls short

The maps, the Atlas and the games are built to be seen. Each offers what it can, such as a list beside each map and a setting in Alarm Fatigue that shows its sounds as text, but some parts still need a mouse or a finger, and a screen reader will not get everything a sighted reader does.

Tell me

If something gets in your way, say what page and what happened through the About page. It goes on the list and gets fixed.
