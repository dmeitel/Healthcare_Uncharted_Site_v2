Collapsed Start

The steadying commit fixed how the board MOVES. This board decides how much board there is. One axis runs through all three concepts: what survives at rest, and what grows on demand. The grain of the collapse is the whole decision: role, line, or family.

Settled, in every concept: the zoom, camera, and floor work from the last commit stays. Show everything is always ONE tap away. A shared ?role= link auto-expands its own path before it lands. The Focused chip keeps naming whatever state the board is in.

The expand moveThe badge is the button: ▸ 6 paths beside the hex. Tap = the next rung up appears with its own badge. Second tap on a grown role's badge could reveal its WHOLE line (the snap we already have).

decks = 44px targets for free

At restThe decks ARE the overview. The far-zoom abbreviation mode stops being needed, because the resting state is already the readable map.
The expand moveTap a deck = the family ladder unfolds in place, full detail, exactly today's tiles. Accordion by default: opening one folds the last, so the board never re-crowds.
The overviewZoom out from an open family and it folds back to its deck. The map you land on is always legible.
On the phoneStrongest of the three. Decks are big targets by nature, one family's ladder fits a phone column, and the accordion keeps tile count low so the zoom floor never fights the fit.
CostMedium. Deck tile renderer plus fold/unfold per family. The compaction layout and the family lanes already exist, this changes what renders per lane, not where lanes live.
RiskCross-family doors are invisible while both families are folded. Mitigation is already built: the doors live in the detail panel, and door chips can ride the unfolded line.

My read

C at rest, A inside an opened family. Decks give a first-timer a map they can read in one glance and give phones their tile budget back. Inside an unfolded family, A's per-role badges handle depth if we want rung-by-rung growth there, or the family just unfolds whole, which is simpler and probably right.

B is the best-looking answer to "where does this lead" and we should NOT build it, because we already shipped it: that fan is what selection does today. The snap is B. The open question was only ever the resting state.

Sequencing stays honest: this is its own phase, after your phone QA of the steadying commit. Nothing from that commit gets thrown away by any of these.

Decision 2026-08-10, revised after the first mock: desktop keeps the CURRENT board. The collapse happens ON the canvas: each family column folds to one hex deck sitting at its entry tier, tap = the column unfolds in place. The scrolling-list mock at /mock/ was rejected for losing the connections, the shared leveling, and the side-by-side comparison; it survives only as a possible phone direction. The live prototype is the real tool with ?decks=1.

Three calls that shape the build:
1 · Accordion strictness: ONE family open at a time, or allow two side by side for comparisons?
2 · Do decks name their entry rung at rest ("starts at CNA"), or stay to name + count?
3 · Does Show everything keep today's full matrix as a mode, or do the decks fully replace the far view?
