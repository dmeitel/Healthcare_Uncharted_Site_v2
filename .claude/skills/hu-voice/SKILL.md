# Healthcare Uncharted voice

One person wrote this site. He ran vents in an ICU, moved through health
administration into clinical informatics, and now manages an informatics
team. The prose should sound like that person has opinions and scars.

This skill extends the HU VOICE KERNEL in the root CLAUDE.md. Where they
overlap, they agree; where this file is stricter (the budgets below), this
file wins for prose work.

## Hard rules

- No em dashes. Anywhere. Body copy, headings, title tags, meta
  descriptions, alt text. Use a colon, a period, or restructure.
- Sentence length varies hard. A 34-word sentence gets followed by a 4-word
  one. Three consecutive sentences within 5 words of each other is a defect.
- Punctuation is present. Commas, semicolons, parentheses. AI prose is
  punctuation-starved and readers clock it.
- Casual transitions only: "So," "Well," "Now,". Never "Furthermore,"
  "Moreover," "In conclusion," "It's worth noting,".
- ALL CAPS for emphasis. Not bold, not italic.
- Analogies come from lived work: clinical practice, books, gaming.
  Sustained across a few sentences, never decorative one-liners.
- Ask a question out loud mid-thought when the argument earns it.
- Lists read like someone talking themselves through a problem, not a
  corporate outline with parallel stems.
- Endings land on dry wit or defiance. Never a sing-song three-part summary.

## Structural budgets, per page

- Negation-contrast: ONE. The "not X, it's Y" / "not just" / "rather than"
  frame is the most-cited AI tell in 2026. Pick the one place it earns its
  keep and rewrite every other instance.
- Parallel triads: ZERO. Especially anaphoric ones. If three items genuinely
  belong together, make them arrive unevenly: two clauses and a fragment, or
  a fourth item that undercuts the first three.
- No rhetorical device reused from another page in this repo. Once is a
  voice. Twice is scaffolding.
- No numbered markers on things that are not sequences.

## Banned vocabulary

delve, straightforward, genuinely, landscape, in today's landscape, robust,
comprehensive, seamless, leverage (verb), navigate (figurative), underscore
(verb), testament, tapestry, crucial, pivotal, vital, "it's worth noting",
"in conclusion", "furthermore", "moreover", "dive into", "unlock", "empower",
"at the end of the day".

## Substance test

Every claim is specific or it is filler. "Real experience" is a category
noun. "Nine years running vents in a Level I ICU" is a fact nobody can copy.
If a sentence could appear unchanged on a competitor's site, it is not
carrying weight.

Do not perform humanness. Forced fragments, sprinkled "look," and "honestly,"
and manufactured casualness read as fake as the slop they replace. The job is
clearing scaffolding so the specific material already in the draft can land.
